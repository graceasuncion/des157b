Proposal

This project explores what happens to cultural identity when language is lost across generations through the lens of the Filipino American experience. Using speculative design, I want to build an interactive narrative where the user is placed in a conversation with a lola in her kitchen. She speaks to you in Tagalog. You barely understand. The experience simulates that quiet, familiar discomfort of not knowing your own heritage language. This project relies most heavily on speculative design, where they’re imagining an experience that makes an invisible, felt reality visible and navigable. It also draws on challenging mental models, pushing back against the idea that language loss is inevitable or acceptable. The emotional arc of the narrative is informed by utopian and dystopian framing: what does it look like if this generation does nothing, versus if they decide to start? The experience is a click-to-advance interactive narrative, where the exchanges begin with a grandmother (lola) speaking in Tagalog, untranslated. The libraries that will be used have not been decided, but I want the libraries to help enhance the experience. I'm uncertain about the tone of the reflection prompt: I want it to feel open and universal, not limited to Filipino Americans, but I also don't want it to feel so broad that it loses specificity. The primary audience for this project is the first-generation, second-generation youth in their late teens and early twenties who feel a disconnect between their family’s heritage and their identity but don’t know where to start. The secondary audience is the people who may be interested in understanding and learning more about this experience amongst immigrant families.
