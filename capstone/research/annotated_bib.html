Annotated Bibliography

"For Some Filipino-Americans, Language Barriers Leave Culture Lost in Translation." KQED, 2 Oct. 2019, www.kqed.org/news/10746111.

The article discusses the firsthand experience of Filipino Americans not being able to understand their Tagalog-speaking grandparents. It was quote this feeling is “a sense of disconnection with culture”, which ties with feelings of regret of not being able to hold meaningful conversations with our relatives. This points out that the article discussed the emotional cost of this language gap between the youth and their elders due to colonization and Filipino immigrant families prioritizing English. The article concludes that language loss is more than a communication barrier, but a conflict of cultural identity and family intimacy. For my project, this article validates these experiences due to the language gap that Filipino Americans experience. It helps me understand that this is a common issue that my capstone hopes to address, and I hope that this may resonate with others who may feel the same.

"Between English and Emptiness." We Are One Humanity, 20 Feb. 2026, www.weareonehumanity.org/writings/between-english-and-emptiness.

This essay examines the pace of heritage language loss in Filipino Americans, which shows how Tagalog disappears across generations faster than other immigrant groups’ native languages. It discusses how this increases the number of mental health issues and strained interpersonal relationships. The essay also mentions how this is a result of rooted colonization, where it was taught to Filipinos that English is the only way to succeed. This source provides the urgency that must be taken on this issue and how the project actually stimulates the experience that a lot of Filipino Americans have, making it a community-wide issue. If the emotional experience of the narrative can reach even a fraction of that population and motivate them to start learning, the project is doing something that matters at a scale beyond design.

Zhang, Jie, and Yaqian Liu. "Exploration of Emotion Perception in Serious Interactive Digital Narrative." National Center for Biotechnology Information, 28 Jun. 2022, www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9347217/

This study examines how emotional arcs in serious interactive digital narratives influence audience engagement and motivation. It was found that the most preferred and effective structure would be a rise, a dip, and an uptick towards the end. This type of emotional arc provides stronger emotional engagement. It’s concluded that designing for emotion keeps in mind how that experience is shaped. This study was useful hast helps me gain an understanding of how I can build the narrative structure for my capstone project and how to engage my audience when interacting with it. This article really solidifies the direction that I want to take for my project of making my audience experience the negative experience of the language gap, but guiding them to view this experience differently.
